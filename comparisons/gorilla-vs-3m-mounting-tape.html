3M VHB Heavy Duty Mounting Tape RP25 — The Industry Standard

$14.95

3M's VHB (Very High Bond) tape line is legendary in industrial and automotive circles. Originally developed to replace rivets and screws in skyscraper curtain-wall construction, VHB tape brings genuine engineering pedigree to the home mounting tape category. The RP25 variant is the consumer-friendly version — 1 inch wide, 60 inches long, and formulated for indoor and outdoor use on a variety of surfaces including painted drywall, metal, glass, and some plastics.

VHB's secret weapon is its viscoelastic acrylic foam core. The foam compresses under load to distribute stress evenly across the bond line, which means it handles vibration and thermal expansion better than solid adhesive tapes. If you're mounting something that will experience movement or temperature swings — like an outdoor security camera bracket — VHB's foam construction gives it an edge in long-term fatigue resistance.

Surface Versatility: Where Each Tape Shines

This is where the two tapes diverge most dramatically. Gorilla's adhesive formula is engineered specifically for rough and uneven surfaces. In our tests, it bonded firmly to brick, rough-sawn wood, concrete block, and even painted stucco within seconds of applying pressure. The tape conforms to surface irregularities instead of bridging over them, which means you get near-total surface contact even on bumpy substrates.

3M VHB, by contrast, performs best on smooth, clean surfaces. On glass, painted drywall, metal, and smooth plastic, it creates a tenacious bond that actually increases in strength over 24–72 hours. On brick or rough masonry, however, VHB's foam core can't compress enough to make full contact with the irregular surface. We saw noticeably weaker adhesion on brick — about 40% less than Gorilla in our peel tests.

Pro Tip: For smooth surfaces like metal or glass, VHB's foam core gives it superior long-term vibration resistance. For brick, wood, stone, or textured drywall, Gorilla's rough-surface formulation is the clear winner.

Instant Bond vs. Cure Time

If you need something to stay put right now, Gorilla wins hands down. Its bond develops almost instantly — press it on for 60 seconds and you can immediately hang a 5-pound object. Full 30-pound strength takes about 24 hours, but you'll see impressive grab within the first minute.

3M VHB requires patience. The initial adhesion is surprisingly light — you can easily peel it off within the first few minutes. Full bond strength develops over 24–72 hours as the acrylic foam flows and wets out the surface at a microscopic level. This is by design: the slow cure allows VHB to be repositioned, which is useful for automotive work where alignment is critical. But if you're hanging a calendar hook and want it to stay now, VHB will frustrate you.

Durability and Outdoor Performance

Both tapes are rated for outdoor use, but they handle the elements differently. After 30 days of direct sun and rain exposure on a south-facing brick wall:

Gorilla: Held firm throughout. No visible adhesive bleed, no edge lifting, no loss of bond strength. The tape yellowed slightly after about 3 weeks of direct sun, but performance was unaffected. On brick, it outperformed every other mounting tape we've tested outdoors.

3M VHB: Also held firm, with zero visible degradation. The acrylic foam maintained its integrity even through 90°F+ afternoons and overnight rain. VHB's wider temperature range (-20°F to 200°F vs Gorilla's 0°F to 120°F) makes it a better choice for extreme climates — think roof-mounted solar light clips or automotive exterior trim in desert heat.

Real-World Applications

Hanging a Wi-Fi extender on a brick wall: Gorilla is the easy winner here. Its rough-surface grip grabs brick mortar and face both, and the instant bond means you don't have to support the extender for an hour while the tape cures.

Securing automotive trim or a dash cam: VHB's foam core absorbs vibration better than solid adhesive. For automotive use — especially on a vehicle that lives outside — VHB's wider temperature tolerance and fatigue resistance make it the better choice.

Mounting a cable raceway to painted drywall: Either tape works great. Gorilla is cheaper and faster. VHB gives you a window to reposition the raceway before the bond sets. We'd grab whichever is on hand.

Outdoor holiday lights to brick or stone: Gorilla all the way. The rough-surface grip is unmatched, and the instant hold means you can clip and move on without waiting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I remove Gorilla mounting tape without damaging paint?

Both tapes create a permanent bond. Removal typically requires heat (hair dryer or heat gun) and gentle peeling at a 180-degree angle. On painted drywall, you may lift paint regardless of which tape you use. Test in an inconspicuous area first.

Will either tape work on stucco or textured walls?

Gorilla's rough-surface formulation handles light-to-medium texture well. For heavy orange-peel or popcorn texture, neither tape will get enough surface contact — you'll need a mechanical fastener.

How long does the bond last outdoors?

Both tapes are UV-stable and waterproof. In our 30-day outdoor test, both held without failure. Gorilla recommends yearly replacement for outdoor applications; 3M rates VHB for multiple years on clean surfaces.

Does either tape work on cold surfaces?

VHB can be applied down to -20°F (though bond strength increases with temperature). Gorilla recommends application above 50°F for best results. For winter outdoor projects, VHB is the safer choice.
