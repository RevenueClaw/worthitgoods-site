🔥 Cooking Performance

Both tallows excel in the kitchen, but they behave differently depending on what you're cooking.

Frying & Roasting

South Chicago's Wagyu tallow has a smoke point around 400°F — comfortably above the 350°F you need for pan-frying and roasting. The higher smoke point means less oxidation and fewer free radicals during cooking. It produces the crispiest roast potatoes, the fluffiest fried chicken, and the most tender scrambled eggs you've ever tasted.

EPIC's bison tallow has a slightly lower smoke point (~375°F) due to its higher CLA content. This is still more than adequate for most cooking applications, but push it past 380°F and you'll start to notice a slightly earthy, gamey aroma — not unpleasant, but distinct.

Flavor Transfer

This is where the two really diverge. Wagyu tallow is remarkably neutral with a mild buttery richness that doesn't overpower other ingredients. It's the perfect all-purpose cooking fat. EPIC's bison tallow has a pronounced savory character — almost like a rich bone broth. It's fantastic for searing steaks (meat on meat action) and seasoning cast iron, but it might be too assertive for delicate baked goods or mild-flavored vegetables.
