Build Quality & Materials

The first thing you notice picking up the CRANACH is the weight. The full aluminum body feels substantial — not heavy, but reassuring. The knurling on the barrel gives you a solid grip even with wet or greasy hands. The pocket clip is spring-loaded and holds tight to a shirt pocket or notebook cover.

The Zonon is noticeably lighter and feels more like a giveaway pen than an EDC tool. The body uses a mix of plastic and thin metal. The clip is a basic wire style that bends under pressure. The screwdriver bits are held in the cap by friction only — they fall out if you shake the pen. It works in a pinch, but it doesn't inspire confidence the way the CRANACH does.

Writing Experience

A multitool pen is still a pen first. The CRANACH uses a standard D1-style ballpoint refill that writes smoothly on everything from sticky notes to cardboard. The ink is dark and consistent — no skipping, no blobs. Replacement refills are easy to find and swap in seconds.

The Zonon uses a two-head bi-directional refill (ballpoint on one end, stylus tip on the other). The writing quality is serviceable but scratchy. The ink runs lighter and the pen needs more pressure to write consistently. After a week of daily use, we noticed skipping near the end of the refill's life.

Durability & Long-Term Use

We carried both pens for two weeks. The CRANACH aluminum body showed minimal wear — a couple scuffs on the pocket clip from rubbing against keys, but the ruler markings stayed legible and the screwdriver bits stayed secure. The spring-clip mechanism is solid; after hundreds of clip/unclip cycles, it still grips with the same tension.

The Zonon didn't fare as well. The printed ruler markings started rubbing off by day three. The friction-fit screwdriver bits dropped out a few times in the bottom of a bag. The wire clip lost tension and now hangs at a slight angle. It still works, but it feels cheaper after a week than it did out of the package.
