What Is an EDC Challenge Coin?

EDC challenge coins serve a different purpose than traditional military challenge coins. While military coins prove membership in a unit or commemorate service, EDC coins are personal talismans. They sit in your pocket, rest on your desk, or get fidgeted with during meetings. They carry meaning — whether that meaning is a stoic reminder to seize the day or a hilarious acknowledgment that sometimes life is a dumpster fire, and that's okay.

A good EDC coin needs three things: a meaningful (or amusing) design, comfortable pocket carry dimensions, and the durability to survive daily handling. Both of these coins deliver on all three, but they go about it completely differently.

Design & Craftsmanship

The Dumpster Fire Coin is the more visually complex of the two. The enamel coloring gives it a vibrant, comic-book quality — the orange flames pop, the silver knight gleams, and the dumpster is unmistakably a dumpster. It's detailed, colorful, and unapologetically goofy. The zinc alloy base gives it good weight for its size, and the silver plating keeps it looking fresh. The double-sided design means you get two pieces of art in one coin — flip it over and you get the sword-in-dumpster scene that's equally detailed.

The Memento Mori Coin takes the opposite approach. It's a single-sided copper piece that wears its handcrafted nature proudly. Because each coin is hand-struck by Shire Post Mint, no two are exactly identical — the strike depth, the edge alignment, the slight off-center impressions are features, not flaws. The copper starts bright and warm, then slowly develops a darker, richer patina over months and years of handling. It's a coin that ages with you, which is deeply appropriate for its message.

Winner for visual impact: Dumpster Fire. The full-color enamel is undeniably striking. But the Memento Mori wins for heirloom potential — that hand-struck copper will only get better with time.

Pocket Feel & Everyday Carry

Both coins are pocket-friendly, but they feel different in the hand. The Dumpster Fire is a touch thicker (0.12" vs ~0.08"), giving it a chunkier presence. It's noticeable in pocket change but not obtrusive. The plastic case it comes in is sturdy enough for desk display or storage, though you wouldn't carry the case in your pocket.

The Memento Mori is thinner but denser — the solid copper has a satisfying heft that belies its slim profile. Coin enthusiasts will appreciate the way real copper warms to body temperature and develops a patina. It slides into a pocket easily and disappears until you pull it out for a reflective moment. The lack of a case means you can carry it raw — which is the whole point of a patina-developing coin.

Winner for EDC comfort: Tie. The Dumpster Fire's thickness gives it presence; the Memento Mori's copper gives it soul. Both carry well.

Over years of pocket carry, the silver plating will eventually wear through to the zinc underneath — that's the nature of plated coins. But at $14.99, you're getting exactly what you pay for: a well-constructed novelty coin that'll last years of desk life and respectable pocket time.

The Memento Mori, being solid copper, is functionally immortal. You could carry it daily for fifty years and it would simply develop a richer, deeper patina. The hand-struck design will never wear off because it's physically pressed into the metal, not printed or plated on top. If durability and longevity matter most, the copper coin is the clear winner on materials alone.

Winner for durability: Memento Mori. Solid copper outlasts plated zinc every time.
