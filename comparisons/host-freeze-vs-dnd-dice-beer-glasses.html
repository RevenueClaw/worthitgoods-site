How They Work — Totally Different Technologies

The Host Freeze glasses use a simple but effective trick: the walls of the plastic pint glass are hollow and filled with a food-safe cooling gel. You pop the glass in the freezer for about two hours, and that gel freezes solid. When you pour in your beer, the frozen gel slowly absorbs heat from the liquid, keeping it cold significantly longer than a standard glass would. The plastic construction means it won't shatter on a patio, and the silicone grip band prevents condensation from making the glass slippery. There's no ice involved, so your beer doesn't get watered down.

The DND Dice glasses go a completely different direction. They're handmade blown glass with polyhedral dice (d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20 — the full set) sealed inside the glass walls during the blowing process. The dice are fully encapsulated, so they never touch your drink. Every glass is unique because no two dice configurations land the same way. They're lighter than they look, and the glass quality is genuinely good — these aren't thin-walled souvenir glasses.

Practical Considerations

Maintenance: Both require hand washing. The Host Freeze glasses need special care — warm water only (hot water can damage the gel seal), and you should dry them before refreezing. The DND Dice glasses are standard hand-wash delicate glassware. Neither is dishwasher safe.

Freezer logistics: The Host Freeze glasses need 2 hours in the freezer before use. If you're hosting a party, you'll want to pop them in well before guests arrive. They also take up freezer space. Not ideal if your freezer is packed.

Glass breakage: The DND Dice glasses are real glass. They'll break if dropped on tile or concrete. The Host Freeze is plastic — it can crack under extreme impact but won't shatter into dangerous shards.
