Real-World Test: Which One Actually Works?

We tested both units on a 2019 Honda CR-V (2.4L 4-cylinder) with a completely dead battery — voltage reading 3.2V, not enough to even trigger the dashboard lights.

NOCO GB40: Connected in about 30 seconds. Pressed the power button, clipped the clamps (spark-proof circuit confirmed correct connection), and the Honda started on the first crank. The GB40 still had 75% charge after the jump. Recharged via USB-C in about 3 hours.

JNC660: Took about 90 seconds to get out of the trunk and wheel over. Connected in 15 seconds. The Honda fired up so fast it was almost anticlimactic — the JNC660 barely noticed it was doing work. The compressor later inflated a flat tire from 15 PSI to 35 PSI in about 4 minutes.

Key takeaway: Both started the car. For the vast majority of drivers, the GB40 does everything you need. The JNC660 is overkill for a 4-cylinder, but it's the only choice if you also need air or work with large engines.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the NOCO Boost Plus start a diesel truck?

Up to 3.0L diesel. That covers smaller diesel cars (VW TDI, Mercedes 250d) and some small tractor diesels, but not full-size pickup diesels like a Duramax 6.6L or a Powerstroke 6.7L. For those, you need the JNC660 or NOCO's bigger GB150.

Will the Jump-N-Carry fit in my trunk?

It will, but it takes up a lot of space. At 18 x 10 x 12 inches and 48 pounds, it's about the size of a medium tool box. Plan for dedicated trunk space. It comes with a handle and wheels on the base, so you can roll it to the vehicle.

How often do I need to charge these?

The JNC660 needs a top-up every 3-4 months if not used. NOCO recommends checking the GB40 every 3 months, and it holds a charge for up to 12 months. The GB40 has a built-in battery level indicator, so you always know where you stand.

Can I leave them in my car in summer heat?

The JNC660 is fine — AGM batteries handle heat and cold well. The NOCO GB40 should not be left in direct sunlight or cars that exceed 140°F (60°C) for extended periods. In practice, under a seat or in a glovebox, it's usually fine, but extreme desert conditions can shorten lithium battery life.

Which one is safer for someone who's never jumped a car?

The NOCO GB40, by a wide margin. Its spark-proof technology means you won't get sparks even if you accidentally touch the clamps together. Reverse polarity protection means if you connect it backward, it simply won't work — no explosion risk. The JNC660 uses standard clamps and requires more care.
